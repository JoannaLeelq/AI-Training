# Safety, Privacy and Security Incident Response

**Incident commander:** CEO/delegate — assign contact  
**Privacy owner:** assign contact  
**Clinical safety lead:** assign contact  
**Customer emergency contacts:** maintain per contract

Treat wrong-person notes, unsafe drafts, unauthorised disclosure, lost recordings, account compromise, ransomware, service failure affecting documentation or supplier incidents as incidents until triaged.

## Immediate actions

1. **Protect people:** if care may be affected, contact the customer's clinical lead immediately and use the approved manual workflow. The software must not delay urgent care.
2. **Contain:** disable affected access/integration/model path as needed; revoke credentials; preserve logs and evidence; do not silently alter records.
3. **Escalate:** notify incident commander, privacy, security and clinical safety owners. Notify the customer promptly under the contract.
4. **Scope:** establish timeline, systems, data/residents, access/export, likely harm, affected care decisions and supplier involvement.
5. **Mitigate:** reduce harm, support record correction/communications as agreed, and coordinate with suppliers.

## External reporting assessment

- Privacy owner and counsel assess Privacy Act/NDB and state/territory duties. Where an eligible breach is likely to cause serious harm, notify OAIC and affected people as required. The NDB assessment period is generally up to 30 days; containment and customer notice start immediately.
- If My Health Record information/system is involved, use its separate reporting pathway.
- Clinical safety and regulatory advisers assess TGA adverse-event, recall or other duties if the product is regulated or exempt as a medical device.
- The aged care customer determines provider reporting duties to the Commission, residents, representatives and other authorities; give them accurate facts promptly.
- Document the rationale for every notification decision.

## Severity

- **S1 Critical:** plausible serious harm, wrong-resident record, active exfiltration, cross-customer exposure or major outage without fallback. Immediate executive/customer escalation.
- **S2 High:** material documentation defect, confirmed exposure or significant service degradation. Same-day escalation.
- **S3 Moderate:** contained issue with no known care impact and limited data risk. Prompt triage and tracked fix.
- **S4 Low:** near miss or usability issue with no material risk. Log and trend.

Keep one incident record with timeline, scope, evidence, decisions, notifications, corrective-action owner and due date. Complete a root-cause review for major incidents and verify the fixes.
